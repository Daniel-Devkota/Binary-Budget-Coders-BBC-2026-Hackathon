  Best option: Playwright demo script
  - chromium.launch() + recordVideo → gives you a .webm of the whole run, no
    screen-recorder needed.
  - Playwright's real cursor is invisible in recordings, so you inject a small
    CSS/JS "fake cursor" overlay (a dot/arrow div) and animate it with easing
    between targets. That's what gives the smooth glide instead of teleporting.
  - Typing: page.type(sel, text, { delay: 60 }) → human-paced keystrokes.
  - Pacing: deliberate waitForTimeout pauses before each click so there's room
    for your voiceover.
  - Output: .webm → ffmpeg to .mp4, drop into any editor, record voice on top.

  Workflow that fits "I tell you what to do"
  1. You give me the script beat-by-beat ("land on home, search for guitar, open
     the map, click a slot, book it").
  2. I write demo/demo.spec.ts with those steps as labeled scenes + timed
     pauses.
  3. Run it, watch the video, you tell me "slower on step 3, zoom the map more"
     — I tweak and re-run. It's deterministic, so re-records are cheap.
  4. You record the voiceover against the final cut.

  Alternatives
  - chrome-devtools MCP (already connected here): I can click/type in your live
    browser and take screenshots, but it captures stills, not video, and the
    cursor movement isn't animated. Good for verifying flows, not for a polished
    demo reel.
  - Screen recording (QuickTime/OBS) while I drive the browser via MCP — real
    screen capture, but jerkier and less repeatable.

  Nice extras once the base works: a caption overlay per scene, a click "ripple"
  animation, slow-mo on key moments, seeded demo data so the content is

  Yes, VO-driven pacing works. You record the narration first, and the cursor
  timing gets derived from your actual audio rather than guessed.

  Two ways to do the sync, from simplest to fanciest:

  1. Per-scene audio (simple, robust — recommended to start)
  You record one audio file per beat (07-map.wav, 08-feed.wav, matching the
  slides already in your demo-video-script.md). ffprobe gives me the exact
  duration of each file, and the script stretches that scene's glides, pauses,
  and typing speed to fill it exactly. If your map narration runs 14.2s, the map
  scene runs 14.2s. Re-record a line, re-run, it re-fits. No transcription
  needed.

  2. Word-level anchors (tighter, more setup)
  One continuous VO file, transcribed with word-level timestamps, so you can
  anchor an action to a specific word — the click lands exactly on "book it,"
  the zoom starts on "all the way down to your own city." That needs a
  transcriber; whisper isn't installed here (ffmpeg/ffprobe are), so it'd mean a
  pip install openai-whisper or an API call. Worth it for the money shots,
  overkill for all nine slides.

  Sensible combo: #1 for everything, #2 for the two or three moments where a
  click needs to hit a word.

  What the timing model actually controls
  Each scene becomes a list of beats — move, dwell, click, type, scroll, zoom —
  with weights instead of fixed durations. Given the scene's audio length, the
  script solves for real durations: long glides get most of the slack, clicks
  stay snappy, and there's a held beat after each click so the eye catches the
  result. Typing speed scales the same way, so a sentence you read slowly gets
  slower keystrokes.
